“We recycle.” Every junk removal company in Winnipeg has that on its website, including us. It is also the easiest claim in this industry to make and the hardest for a customer to check, because you watch a trailer drive away and that is the last you ever see of any of it. So here is what actually happens to the load.

The sort happens twice

The first sort is at your place, while we load. Metal goes in one area of the trailer, anything donatable is kept clean and undamaged, and electronics are kept separate rather than buried under a wardrobe.

The second sort is back at the shop, where the load gets broken out properly into its destinations. Doing it in two passes is the difference between real diversion and a token gesture — once a mattress has been crushed under rubble, nobody is recovering anything from it.

Where each category goes

What it is Where it actually ends up
Furniture and household goods in usable condition Donated to local reuse. Clean, working, and structurally sound is the bar — charities are not a landfill with extra steps, and sending them junk costs them money to dispose of.
Appliances, BBQs, bed frames, filing cabinets Scrap metal. This is the category with real recovery value, which is exactly why it is worth our time to separate it.
Fridges, freezers, air conditioners Handled separately because of the refrigerant. These cannot simply be crushed with everything else.
Computers, TVs, monitors, small electronics Provincial e-waste recycling, which exists precisely so this material does not go in the ground.
Clean concrete, brick and patio stone Crushed and reused as fill where the load is clean. Contaminate it with drywall and plastic and that route closes — which is why we ask you to keep it separate.
Yard waste and clean wood Composting or wood recycling, depending on condition and whether it is treated.
Cardboard, paper, clean plastics Standard recycling streams.
Mattresses Deconstructed for the steel and foam where a facility is taking them; landfill where it is not. This one genuinely varies.
Mixed, wet, broken or contaminated material Landfill. Honestly.

The part other companies leave out

Not everything gets diverted. A hot tub shell is landfill. Soiled mattresses are landfill. Broken particle-board furniture is landfill, because nobody wants it and it falls apart on contact with moisture. Wet, mixed renovation debris is largely landfill.

Anyone telling you they divert 100 per cent of everything is either counting creatively or lying outright. We would rather give you a number we can stand behind and be specific about what falls outside it.

Why we bother, honestly

Partly because it is the right thing to do. We are not going to pretend that is the whole story though.

The landfill charges by the tonne. Every tonne we divert into donation, scrap or recycling is a tonne we do not pay to bury, and scrap metal is worth money rather than costing it. That saving is a large part of why our prices work. Sorting is not charity here; it is the business model. Being straight about that seems more useful than performing environmental virtue we would not otherwise afford.

Ask for proof

On commercial jobs we provide scale tickets and disposal documentation as standard, so a property manager or contractor can show where material actually went. If a hauler cannot produce that, treat their recycling claim as marketing.

What you can do to improve the outcome

  • Keep donatables dry and separate. A good couch left in the rain overnight is no longer a good couch.
  • Do not pile rubble on top of everything. It crushes the recoverable material underneath.
  • Pull out the hazardous items yourself. Paint, solvents, chemicals, pesticides, propane, fuel and oil are things we cannot take at all, and one leaking can contaminate a whole load that would otherwise have been recyclable.
  • Tell us what is in there. A load we know about is a load we can route properly.
